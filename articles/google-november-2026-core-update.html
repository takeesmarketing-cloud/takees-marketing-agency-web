Google began rolling out its November 2026 core update on 10 November, at about 11:36 pm India time. If your enquiry calls or website visits look different this week, the update could be one reason. A broken form or a seasonal dip could also be behind it. This guide helps you tell the difference before you change anything.

Many owners only notice an update when leads slow down. A clinic in Kozhikode or a furniture store in Whitefield rarely follows Google announcements. Below, you will find what Google has confirmed, how to check your numbers, and what to avoid.

What Is a Google Core Update?

A core update is a broad change to the systems Google uses to rank search results. Google releases several of these each year. They are not aimed at any single website, city or industry.

Picture a popular food guide that ranks the best biryani spots in Kochi. Every few months, its editors rethink how they judge. Maybe fresh ingredients and honest reviews now count for more. Your restaurant serves the same food as before, yet a newer place moves above you. Nobody fined you. The list was simply judged again.

A core update works the same way. Google reassesses how well each page answers a search. Some pages that were under-rewarded rise, and others slip down. According to Google, a page that performs less well after a core update has nothing wrong with it.

Spam updates work in a different way. They target sites breaking Google’s spam policies, such as sites buying links. A core update judges overall quality and usefulness instead.

Which Websites and Businesses Could Be Affected?

Any site that appears in Google Search can move during a core update. Google itself says most sites don’t need to worry, and many won’t notice anything.

Google’s quality guidelines hold topics affecting health, money or big decisions to a higher standard. Here is how that applies to common business types.

  • Clinics and healthcare websites. A dental clinic page with no named dentist or qualifications gives readers little reason to trust it.
  • Real estate businesses. Buyers are making large money decisions. Vague listings and copied area descriptions don’t help them.
  • Educational websites. Coaching centres often leave old fees or last year’s batch dates online. Fresher, clearer pages can overtake them.
  • Restaurants and local services. These rely heavily on Google Maps, but your website still supports how Google understands your business.
  • Ecommerce websites. A product page that copies the manufacturer’s description reads the same as hundreds of other stores.

Thin or repetitive content is usually the most exposed. Picture forty “plumber in [town]” pages where only the town name changes.

Websites that show genuine expertise are better placed. A physiotherapist who explains recovery steps from real patient sessions offers something a copied article can’t.

What You Should Avoid During the Google Update

Rankings often bounce around during a rollout before they settle. Quick reactions now usually cause more trouble than the update.

Reacting to daily ranking swings

A drop on day three can reverse by day ten. Judging your site on one bad morning leads to rushed decisions.

Deleting pages in a hurry

A page that slipped for one search may still bring calls from others. Removing it also throws away the links and history it has built up.

Changing everything at once

Rewrite pages, switch themes and move URLs in one week, and you will never know what helped. Keep a simple log of any change you must make.

Buying backlinks to recover

Google’s spam policies forbid buying links that pass ranking value. Paid links can add a second problem on top of the first.

Assuming Google caused every drop

Traffic also falls when a tracking code breaks, a contact form fails, or seasonal demand slows. Rule out these simple causes before anything else.

How to Check Whether Your Website Lost Rankings

Google’s own core update guidance suggests a simple before-and-after comparison. Here is how to apply it, step by step.

  1. Mark the start date. Note 10 November 2026 in your Search Console and GA4 reporting, or in a shared spreadsheet your team checks.
  2. Save your baseline. Export Search Console data for 3 to 9 November, the seven days before the rollout began.
  3. Wait for the rollout to finish. Check the Search Status Dashboard for the official end date. Google then recommends waiting at least one more full week.
  4. Compare clicks, impressions and average position. In the performance report, open the date filter and choose Compare. Set your post-update week against 3 to 9 November.
  5. Find the pages and queries that moved. Sort the Pages and Queries tabs by change in clicks. Look for patterns, like blog posts falling while service pages hold.
  6. Check whether the change is seasonal. Compare the same weeks from last year. In Kerala, the Sabarimala season starts in mid-November and shifts demand for travel and hospitality. Online stores will also see Black Friday swings on 27 November.
  7. Look for tracking errors and recent site changes. Confirm your GA4 tag fires on every page. Ask your developer what changed on the site in the past month.

A slip from position 3 to 5 is very different from a fall from 4 to 30. Small moves often need no action, while big drops on key pages deserve a closer look.

If your customers include Malayali families in the Gulf, check the countries filter too. This update is global, so searches from Dubai or Doha can shift as well.

How to Improve Your Website After Losing Rankings

No single technical fix reverses a core update drop. Google’s advice is to focus on helpful, reliable content written for people. In practice, work through these areas.

  • Be more useful than the top results. Read the first three results for your main keyword. Ask honestly what your page offers that they don’t.
  • Add genuine experience. Share what your team really sees, like questions patients repeat or mistakes first-time buyers make.
  • Update outdated details. Old prices, expired offers and 2023 dates tell readers a page has been forgotten.
  • Show who stands behind the content. Add author names, qualifications, your address and real photos of your team.
  • Fix speed and mobile problems. Many customers search on a phone using mobile data. Test key pages with Google’s free PageSpeed Insights tool.
  • Strengthen internal links. Connect related pages so visitors can reach your best content.
  • Match each page to its search. A page targeting “dentist in Kannur” should clearly be about a dentist in Kannur.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile accurate. Hours, phone number, services and photos should match your website.

Google says improvements can take several months to show, and some sites only recover at the next core update. Even good changes carry no guarantee, because no page holds a fixed position.

What Kerala and Bangalore Businesses Should Check

Local businesses appear in regular results and the map pack. Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and your website supports two of those.

  • Name, address and phone match everywhere. Check your website, Google Business Profile, Justdial, IndiaMART and Facebook. Use one spelling, such as “Bengaluru” or “Bangalore”, and stick to it.
  • Your Google Business Profile is complete. Confirm your main category, services, photos and opening hours. Add special hours for Christmas and New Year now.
  • Reviews are recent and answered. Reply to every review, good or bad, within a few days. Never pay anyone for fake reviews.
  • Each location has its own page. A business serving Kochi, Thrissur and Kozhikode needs three pages with real local details, not one page copied three times.
  • Local visibility is tracked. Search your main service plus your area on a phone. Note where you appear in the map pack and the regular results.
  • Content speaks to local customers. Mention the neighbourhoods you serve, such as Indiranagar or Edappally. Say which languages your staff speak, like Malayalam, Kannada or Tamil.

Frequently Asked Questions

When did the Google November 2026 Core Update start?

It started on 10 November 2026 at 10:06 am US Pacific time. In India, that was about 11:36 pm the same night.

How long will the update take to finish?

Google says the rollout may take up to two weeks. Using the full time, it would end around 24 November. Google has not confirmed an end date yet.

Is a Google core update a penalty?

No, a core update is not a penalty. Core updates never target specific sites. A drop means Google now ranks other pages higher for those searches.

My website traffic dropped. Should I change my website?

Not yet, because the rollout is still running. Wait until it ends, plus one more week, then compare your data and fix what truly dropped.

How long does recovery take?

There is no fixed timeline for recovery. Google says improvements can take months, and some sites only recover at the next core update.

Can a Google core update affect local businesses?

Yes, local business websites can move like any other site. A stronger website also supports how Google judges your business in the map pack.

What Should You Do Next?

  • Save 3 to 9 November 2026 as your baseline week.
  • Wait for Google to confirm the rollout is complete, then wait one more week.
  • Compare pages and queries, and rule out seasonal dips and tracking errors first.
  • Improve pages that lost ground with real expertise and current details.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile and contact details accurate everywhere.
